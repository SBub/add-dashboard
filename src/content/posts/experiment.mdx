Product engineering taught me one habit: a hypothesis is a starting point, not a decision.

Product discovery is that habit:

1. Find a problem worth solving, and who has it.
2. Talk to those people before building anything.
3. Fake the solution: a landing page with a waitlist, a fake door, or Wizard of Oz (doing the work by hand behind a real interface).
4. Bring people to it. Without visitors, the test can't answer anything.
5. Build the smallest real version for the people who said yes.
6. Watch whether they come back. If they don't, go back to step 1.

The formula: a hypothesis, a cheap test, a decision. Keep going, change direction, or stop.

Hypotheses are great. They're how every product starts. But a hypothesis that hasn't been tested is just an opinion, and building on opinions is expensive.
